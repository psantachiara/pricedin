How to read this

Prices are daily closes adjusted for splits and dividends, updated each weekday evening from Yahoo Finance. Market value multiplies each close by today’s share count, so older values are approximate.

An announcement is placed on the first trading day on or after its date, or the day after if it came out after the market closed. Its move runs from the previous close to the close at the end of the reaction window.

“Against benchmark” subtracts the benchmark’s move over the same days, a simple way to separate a stock’s reaction from the market’s. Earnings, macro news and other stories still land on the same days, so a move shows what followed an announcement, not what it caused.

Which stocks an announcement touches is an editorial choice, recorded in data/events.json. Hover a company name beside the rows to see why its news is tagged to the stocks it is. The industry total combines only the companies selected above.

Clicking a company chip hides its line; clicking a framing or topic chip below the prices hides it. Shift-click any of them to show only that one, which is the quicker way in when something is rare. The framing chips are grouped by family — danger, capability or bubble, no stance — and spend no colour of their own, so that the hue on the timeline belongs to the subject.

Each announcement is coded on three axes: whether it is about AI at all, what it is about, and how it characterises AI. The last two are multi-label, so a funding round reported with alarm stays a funding round. Dots beside a framing tag mark its intensity, and “Filter by AI as” sets how much of it has to be present before the announcement counts as carrying it — at Dominant only a handful of unmistakable items survive, at Mentioned every passing reference does.

Framing is assigned by a language model applying the same written codebook a human coder would use, and every announcement records which coder produced its labels — the model, a person, or the keyword matcher that came before. That matcher under-reported framing and mistook ordinary news vocabulary for claims about AI, which is why it was replaced. The model pass has not yet been checked against a fully hand-coded sample, so framing counts here are well-founded but provisional.

The source coverage strip under the timeline shows how much of each week the news source actually reaches. Where it is short or red, the record is thin or missing, so a quiet stretch there means no coverage rather than no news. An amber week rests on a single feed, which is a narrower view than the rest of the timeline; hover any week to see which feeds it came from.
